Hate hides inside fake stories.
Teach your model to say why.

HateMirage is a shared task on explainable Faux Hate — comments that are both hateful and built on debunked claims. Don't just flag them: name the Target, the Intent, and the Implication behind every one.

The problem

A clean-looking comment can still be a weapon.

No slur, no profanity — a keyword filter reads it as harmless. But the premise is fabricated and the hostility is real. HateMirage decomposes exactly that gap. Try it on a real (masked) example from the corpus:

“The coronavirus is just a scam planned by [Political Party] and [Country] to fool us all.”
Target

[Political Party], [Country] — the entities the comment is actually directed against, even though the sentence reads as a claim about a virus.

Intent

To link [Political Party] and [Country] to a COVID-19 conspiracy theory — the motive a surface classifier never sees.

Implication

Could fuel public anger against [Political Party] and [Country] — the downstream social cost of letting this narrative spread.

Two sub-tasks, one corpus

Locate the target. Then explain the motive and the harm.

Both sub-tasks run on the same HateMirage comments, so a single system can be built to handle the full pipeline — or teams can enter either one on its own.

Task A

Target Identification

Given a Faux Hate comment, extract the entity or community it's actually directed against — even when the attack is implied rather than stated outright. Output is a short text span.

An accessible entry point for teams new to explanation-based tasks.

InputFaux Hate comment
OutputTarget span
MetricSBERT + ROUGE-L
Task B

Intent & Implication Generation

Given the same comment, generate two free-text explanations: the Intent behind it and its likely Implication. A structured generation problem, not classification — and the part of HateMirage that current models struggle with most.

InputFaux Hate comment
OutputIntent + Implication text
MetricSBERT + ROUGE-L (avg.)
The corpus

4,530 comments, sourced from debunked claims.

Built from widely debunked claims on fact-checking platforms, traced to the YouTube comment sections discussing them on international English news channels — English and Hindi-English code-mixed text included. Full construction details are in the HateMirage dataset paper (LREC 2026) ↗.

Evaluation

Scored for meaning, not just word overlap.

Sentence-BERT similarity

Captures semantic agreement between a predicted explanation and the reference — two correct explanations can be worded differently but still mean the same thing.

ROUGE-L F1

Measures lexical and structural overlap, complementing the semantic score. For Task B, both metrics are computed on Intent and Implication separately, then averaged.
