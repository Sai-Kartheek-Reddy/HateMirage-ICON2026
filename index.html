Hate hides inside fake stories.
Teach your model to say why.

HateMirage is a shared task on explainable Faux Hate — comments that are both hateful and built on debunked claims. Don't just flag them: name the Target, the Intent, and the Implication behind every one.

The problem

A clean-looking comment can still be a weapon.

No slur, no profanity — a keyword filter reads it as harmless. But the premise is fabricated and the hostility is real. HateMirage breaks that gap into three questions: who is actually being attacked, why, and what harm it could do. Try it on a real (masked) example from the corpus:

“[Religious Group] men are trapping innocent [Community] women in fake love to convert them.”
Target — who is attacked

[Religious Group] — the community the comment is actually directed against. A model that misses this can't tell who's being harmed, which is the first thing a moderator or researcher needs to know.

Intent — why it was said

To portray [Religious Group] as deceptive and predatory, reinforcing a fabricated conversion narrative. This is the motive behind the comment — the part a surface-level classifier never sees, because there's no slur to catch.

Implication — what it could cause

Could deepen mistrust between communities and normalise religious profiling. This is the downstream harm — why the comment matters beyond the sentence itself, and what moderation is ultimately trying to prevent.

Shared task

What participants will do

Both tasks run on the same HateMirage comments, so a single system can be built to handle the full pipeline — or teams can enter either one on its own.

Task A

Target Identification

Given a Faux Hate comment, extract the entity or community it's actually directed against — even when the attack is implied rather than stated outright. Output is a short text span.

An accessible entry point for teams new to explanation-based tasks.

Task B

Intent & Implication Generation

Given the same comment, generate two free-text explanations: the Intent behind it and its likely Implication. A structured generation problem, not classification — and the part of HateMirage that current models struggle with most.

The corpus

4,530 comments, sourced from debunked claims.

Built from widely debunked claims on fact-checking platforms, traced to the YouTube comment sections discussing them on international English news channels — English and Hindi-English code-mixed text included. Full construction details are in the HateMirage paper, accepted at LREC 2026 ↗.

Evaluation

Scored for meaning, not just word overlap.

Sentence-BERT similarity

Captures semantic agreement between a predicted explanation and the reference — two correct explanations can be worded differently but still mean the same thing.

ROUGE-L F1

Measures lexical and structural overlap, complementing the semantic score. For Task B, both metrics are computed on Intent and Implication separately, then averaged.
